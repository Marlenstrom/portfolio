Company status

Metrics and guidance for HR and health and safety

2025–2026 · Product design · User research

A clearer way for managers to understand key company metrics, follow progress and see what to do next.

Role
  • Lead product designer: insight, concept, UX/UI and testing
Delivered
  • A shared dashboard for sickness absence, holiday and incidents
  • Goal setting, guidance and empty states
  • Customer research and usability testing
Test result
  • Every participant found sickness absence and set a goal

The problem

Important metrics lived on an outdated index page that no longer fitted the direction of the product. The information was dense, fragmented and often required users to open several tools to understand how their company was doing.

The direction

The cards are designed to give managers in small and medium-sized businesses a quick, understandable view of key health and safety areas. The aim is to help them decide what needs attention—not to turn the page into another reporting tool.

Design principles

These principles helped us decide what to leave out as much as what to include.

  • Give an overview, not a full analysis.
  • Support everyday management decisions, not reporting or deep exploration.
  • Use few choices and a clear hierarchy, so the information works without prior knowledge.
  • Create one useful experience for most small and medium-sized businesses, without extensive customisation.

Starting with what customers need

We asked customers which information was most useful to follow. Sickness absence and trends, incidents and measures were the clearest priorities. This gave us a focused starting point instead of rebuilding every metric from the old page.

From information to action

Each card shows the current status, change over time and the details that matter most. Users can switch to practical guidance, set a goal and follow progress without leaving the overview.

The same cards also appear on the home page, so users can scan the status quickly and open the full view when they need more detail.

What the usability test showed

Participants understood the overview and completed the tasks with a high success rate. Everyone found sickness absence and set a goal. The split between short- and long-term absence, self-certified and doctor-certified absence, and the connection between status and goals were especially useful.

The test also showed where the experience can improve. The label “Incidents” was not clear to everyone, and insight alone was not always enough to make the next step obvious.

Next steps

The strongest opportunity is to turn a useful overview into practical decision support—without adding back the complexity we removed.

  • Clarify terms such as “Incidents” with better microcopy and context.
  • Make recommendations and next actions more specific to the current situation.
  • Explore links to related reports, small alerts and relevant integrations.

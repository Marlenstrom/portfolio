Employment contract

HR processes in the customer portal, web app

2026 · Product design · Concept

I'm designing how employment contracts work in Avonova's new customer portal, an HR tool that has to be simple for small businesses and still work for larger ones. I lead the concept, the UX and the product decisions.

Role
  • Lead product designer: concept, UX and product decisions
Delivered
  • A concept and prototype the team agreed on
  • Requirements document checked against the design
Status
  • In progress: tested internally, customers next

The problem

Small businesses need a correct employment contract, but the contract usually ends up as a PDF in a drawer, cut off from everything else about the employee. When the job changes, nobody knows what applies.

We had to decide where the contract belongs, how it connects to position, salary and workplace, and what happens when the employment changes. The solution had to help with legal requirements without feeling legal or heavy.

A contract is more than a PDF

This became the starting point for the whole design. The contract is part of the employee's employment record, not a separate document. It shows what was agreed, and it gives the product data it can use to follow up later.

Three design decisions

I tested the direction early with an HR adviser. Their input led to standard templates, addendums for special terms, and keeping the contract with the employee.

  • The contract lives under the employee's employment, not in a separate archive.
  • The user describes the change, and the system recommends the right action.
  • Signed documents are never changed. The current terms and the history are kept together.

Creating a new contract

A new contract starts from a template, with company details filled in ahead of time. If there is an offer letter, its terms are read in. The user only answers what is still missing.

The templates are made to cover what the law requires. Before signing, the user sees anything that may be missing and decides what to do about it.

Handling changes

When something changes, the user does not pick a document type. They describe what is happening, and the system suggests the right documentation: an addendum, a new contract or a logged correction.

Working with product and development

I work closely with the product manager and the developer who builds the backend. We worked through the design together while the requirements document (PRD) was being written, and solved conflicts and open questions as they came up.

When the requirements document was ready, I compared it with the design, screen by screen. We agreed on each difference together and updated both.

Status and next steps

This is work in progress. We have tested the concept internally, and the next step is testing it with customers. The concept and the requirements document describe the first version, which is next in line to be built.
